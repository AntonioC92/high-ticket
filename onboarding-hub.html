📞 Coaches Communication Guidelines and Call Etiquette Confirmed

Tone-and-conduct manual for talking to students. Last updated 11 Jun 2026.

As a representative of High Ticket, coaches communicate professionally, respectfully, and authoritatively, without ever making a student feel insulted or inconvenienced. Students invested significantly and deserve respect and dignity, while coaches also need to convey authority and teach students to be CEOs of their own businesses.

1. Lead With Professional Respect
✅ "I'm going to challenge that assumption because the data doesn't support it."
❌ "That's just wrong, you're overthinking this."
2. Keep Communication Public by Default
✅ Redirect DMs into the shared channel
❌ Answer fully in DMs, bypassing channels
3. Model High-Quality Questions
✅ "How many vendors did you contact, what response rate, what changed after #5?"
❌ "Just keep reaching out, it'll work eventually."
4. Clear Responsiveness Boundaries
✅ "I'll review this during support hours tomorrow."
❌ Immediate reply at midnight
5. Reinforce Ownership & Execution
✅ "Once you've implemented the changes, post the update and we'll refine."
❌ Re-strategizing when nothing's changed yet
6. Run Tight, Professional Calls
✅ "We have 15 minutes: review metrics, one decision, next actions."
❌ "What do you feel like talking about today?"
7. Calm Authority Under Stress
✅ "Let's separate emotion from mechanics and look at the numbers."
❌ "Yeah, I get it, this part always sucks."
8. Protect Confidentiality
✅ "Everyone's timeline is different, focus on your controllables."
❌ "Other students at your stage are already seeing sales."
9. Service Without Over-Serving
✅ "My role: clearest path forward. Your role: execute."
❌ "I'll stay on top of you and make sure this gets done."
10. Correct Behavior Early
✅ "Quick note: let's keep questions in the main channel."
❌ Public callout or passive aggression

11. When students question your experience

✅ "I'm trained and specialised in our unique High Ticket strategy. I've worked with dozens/hundreds of students to help them build, launch, and grow. I'm going to do the same with you."
❌ "No, I don't have ecommerce experience."

Call no-show protocol

  • 3 min past start: tag the student in their Slack channel, share the meeting link
  • 6–7 min past, no word: call via Aloware before leaving the call (ping Veronika if no Aloware access)
  • Still no answer: leave the call, send a friendly reschedule message with your Calendly link, and keep the tone non-judgmental

Applies to all call types: Niche Selection, Tech Setup, Ad Setup, bonus coaching calls.

🎬 New Coach Loom Walkthrough Confirmed

Not a video to watch: a Loom you record and submit by end of Week 3. A gut-check, not an exam. Version 1.0, Owner: Coaching Team.

Purpose: before you get your first student, the team wants to hear you walk the student journey out loud, in your own words. ~5–10 min, screen + camera on, read each question aloud and answer free-flow, no slides, no word-for-word notes. Send the Loom link to your lead in #coaches-cohort when done.

Not graded on exact wording or technical depth. They want clear thinking and the right instincts, not perfect recall.

🎯 Niche Selection Call: Stage 1 Confirmed

The single most detailed, script-level SOP in the library (v1.1, Owner: Coaching Team). This is Call 1 with Incubator+ students, the call Coaches Month One Week 3 has you running solo for the first time. It sets the entire store direction and the coach-student relationship.

Purpose & Trigger

Get the student from three shortlisted niches to one confirmed niche with a strategic angle in 30–45 minutes, while building trust and emotional safety for the next 60 days. Triggers once the student has posted their top 3 niches in Slack AND booked Call 1 via Calendly.

Before the call: AI prep workflow

Paste the student's shortlisted niches into a custom GPT (a "Clear Concise English Rewriter" prep prompt) that ranks the niches on profitability/suppliers/AOV/Shopping & Meta suitability, recommends 5 product categories per niche, generates 20 branded niche combinations, 20 store-name ideas, and coach talking points. Output feeds directly into Phase 3 below.

Phase 1: Open the Call (~3-5 min)

Solve any audio/tech friction warmly, never make the student feel stupid (AirPods often hijack audio, so disconnect Bluetooth). Then spend 2-3 min genuinely getting to know them: background, current situation, why e-commerce. Rachel's training is explicit this isn't small talk; it's the relationship that carries the next 60 days.

Phase 2: Review Their Top 3 (~5-10 min)

Listen first, don't lead with your recommendation. Ask how they found each niche, notice which one they're emotionally attached to (hardest to steer away from). Then honestly walk pros/cons of each with specific framing, not generic: e.g. Smart Tech's high returns crush newbie confidence, or Beauty/supplements are tricky for ad-platform compliance.

Phase 3: Deliver Your Recommendation (~10-12 min), the "combinative angle"

Don't just pick one of their 3. Widen it into a 4-5 category store absorbing what they liked from the others:

  1. A core category (chosen niche's anchor, high price point, strong demand)
  2. A high-intent emergency category (EV chargers, pool cleaners, water filtration)
  3. An evergreen/fitness-style category (never goes out of style)
  4. A crossover category (pulls in appeal from another of their top 3)
  5. An upgrade/aspirational category (solar batteries, smart automation)
"Every category here should sit on a nice fine line between nice-to-have and 'I really need this product.'"

Pre-empt emotional resistance by naming their favourite pick directly if you steered away from it, then ask "What's your take?" Don't move on until they verbally agree.

Phase 4: Business Basics Checklist (~5-8 min)

Four-point check: LLC (yes, confirm EIN+address; no, send Doola link, 7-14 days), business bank account, business phone (recommend OpenPhone/Quo), store name (commit to sending 10-15 options by end of day). ⚠️ Never give legal/financial advice; redirect LLC/bank questions to Doola.

Phases 5 & 6: Next Steps, Emotional Check-In, Close (~6-10 min)

Set the timeline expectation honestly (3-4 weeks if student-led decisions stay quick; indecision on small things like colours is what actually stretches it). Ask for an honest temperature check even if things sound fine; probe once more if they say "it's fine." Affirm something specific they're doing right. Close with warmth: "If anyone or anything on the team frustrates you, just tell me, I've got your back."

⭐ Closing hard gate: within 5 minutes of the call ending

Tag @support-team with niche confirmed, LLC status, and store name direction (or "shortlist coming end of day") before you walk away from your desk. Separate from the post-call summary: the summary is for the student, this tag is for Support (the gatekeeper of the tech form). If you only have time for one post-call action, do this one.
👋 @support-team, [Student Name] is ready for IT/Tech Setup. - Niche confirmed: [niche + widened categories] - LLC status: [in place / Doola pending / no LLC yet] - Store name direction: [brand name OR "shortlist coming end of day"] Please send the combined Tech Setup + DFY form when you're ready. Thanks!

Objection scripts (selected)

  • "I really wanted to do [favourite niche] on its own." Say: "Totally hear you, that interest stays in the store. I want to keep you in [their niche] AND give you a few neighbouring categories so you're not fighting in a sea of look-alikes."
  • The "Niche-Switcher" persona (mid-call change requests, avoidance dressed as strategy). Say: "Hold up. The DAC list you saw is research-driven, your niche was vetted on supplier viability and ad performance data. Let's lock this selection in and revisit only if we have real performance data telling us otherwise."
  • Legal/tax/financial question. Say: "Honestly that's a Doola question, I'm not the right person to give you legal advice. Let's get you in their channel."

🧩 Ad Campaign Audit: Stage 7 (PMax Campaign Check-in SOP) Confirmed

Runs 24-48 hours post-campaign-launch, once the student is out of "GMC Jail" and the PMax campaign is live. Goal: technical audit of the Ads account, verify tracking health, and enforce a strict hands-off period so machine learning can take over. Version 1.0, Owner: Coaching Team.

Technical audit

Transitions the student from launching their first campaign to building a long-term, sustainable business. 🚨 Google Merchant Center Suspension | Resolution SOP Confirmed

The most cross-referenced doc in the whole library. Two halves: a decision-tree for what to do the moment GMC suspends, plus an exhaustive pre-submission compliance checklist meant to prevent suspension in the first place.

Core Principle

Avoid prolonged troubleshooting. If a suspension is rejected once and the reason is unclear, repeated appeals will likely fail; move quickly to alternatives rather than getting stuck in verification loops. Target resolution time: 1.5–2 weeks maximum.

The Decision Tree

ScenarioAction
New suspensionFull compliance check, fix issues, submit identity verification (wait 5-7 days)
First rejectionForward to Store Build Team, complete rebuild (new related domain, visually different store, updated policies), new GMC + Ads accounts
Second rejectionStop all Google attempts, pivot to Meta Ads (primary alt.) / Bing Ads (secondary)
Unclear reasonDo NOT investigate further, move to rebuild immediately

The #1 suspension trigger: address/name mismatch

Business name and address must match exactly across the EIN notice, Shopify footer, LLC docs, and GMC. Brand name is used without "LLC" everywhere except the footer (footer shows LLC name only; policies show both, e.g. "Epic Game Rooms operated by Two More, LLC"). Shipping times (handling 1-3 days / transit 5-8 days / total 6-11 days) must also match identically across Shopify policy pages, the Shopify shipping profile, and GMC.

Misrepresentation: the hardest suspension type to resolve

Golden rule: every claim on the store must be 100% true and verifiable. Common triggers: "Free Shipping on All Orders" when it only applies above a threshold (say exactly what's true instead); an advertised price-match guarantee with no real process behind it; fake urgency/countdown timers; inflated crossed-out "original prices" never actually charged; unbacked guarantees ("Lifetime Warranty").

Rule of thumb: if a Google reviewer visited the store and tested every claim, would they all hold up? If not, remove or reword before submitting for verification.

⚠️ Difficult Student, No Shows & Requesting Refunds Confirmed

One of the most important SOPs to internalize: this is the doc behind Loom Walkthrough gut-check Q4 ("a student goes quiet, no-shows, or talks refund, how do you handle it?").

No-show timeline

3 min past start: tag student in Slack, share the meeting link. 6-7 min, no word: call via Aloware. Still nothing: leave the call, send a short kind follow-up, offer the reschedule link. Reschedule window: 48 hrs prior. No-show = forfeited call. Two no-shows in a row with no communication: escalate to Onboarding Team.

Life happens: acknowledge it, don't fix it

When a student flags a personal crisis, resist the instinct to solve it. Don't offer a specific extension length, don't offer a pause (paused members rarely come back), don't probe for details, don't promise a timeline. Good first reply: "Hey [name], really sorry you're dealing with this. Handle what you need to handle, I'm here when you're ready to pick back up." Still 🎫 the channel the same day to loop Aaron in.

Retention: catching problems early

Aaron's team saved ~$105K in a single week keeping 98% of students who called in, but most of those 25 near-losses were preventable if caught earlier by the coach. Five risk signals: health/family crisis, time constraints, financial hardship, misunderstanding the program, coaching/support issues. In every case: extensions over pauses, flag early, document everything.

Refund/cancel requests: the 🎫 trigger

React with 🎫 on any message in the student's channel (or forward a DM into the channel first). Within seconds: Aaron is added, a message posts from Olha, an Intercom ticket is created. For refund requests specifically, the automation also sends the student their call booking link immediately.

Keep coaching them. A refund request isn't the end. Great coaching, and not making them feel awkward for asking, is often exactly what changes their mind.

💬 Coach Scripts: Common Student Questions Confirmed

Word-for-word answers to the questions that come up over and over. Worth memorizing before live student calls, likely to come up in week one.

"Do you have ecommerce experience?"

"I'm trained and specialised in our unique High Ticket strategy. I've worked with over 100 students to help them build, launch, and grow their e-commerce stores and get to their first sale. I'm going to do the same with you."

Never answer "no": what they're really asking is "can you actually help me win?"

"How many millionaires has the program made?"

"We don't track an exact 'millionaires' number, but from our 2025 internal survey: among students in the program 6+ months who successfully launched and marketed, average monthly sales were $55,000 (~$650K/year annualized), from 299 students, self-reported. Results vary, but the program builds a clear path for students who show up and do the work."

⚠️ Watch out

These are sales (revenue), not profit. Never let it become "students make $55K/month." Always keep the qualifiers attached: self-reported, filtered group, not a guarantee.

"What's the guarantee mine will be successful and unique?"

"Those wins are proof the system works. Yours won't be a copy. Your niche, products, and brand make it its own store. I can't promise an outcome, no honest coach can, but I can promise we'll run the strategy that's gotten those results, together."

⚠️ Watch out

Never say "guaranteed" or put a promised outcome in writing. That's the line that shows up in a refund dispute.
